The Stream

What Is the Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream?

The Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream is a collaboration between the Alberta Advantage Immigration Program and approved designated agencies. The agencies assess entrepreneurial readiness, review business plans, and recommend the graduates they judge ready. Alberta then scores recommended candidates on a 200 point grid and invites the highest ranking to submit a Business Application.

The designated agency does more than stamp a letter. It reviews your plan against market need, your path to customers, your partnerships and your financial plan, and it provides a written assessment report that goes in with your Business Application. Choosing and approaching the right agency is the first real step of this stream.

Eligibility

Who This Stream Fits, And Who It Does Not

You Likely Qualify If

  • You completed a degree at a post secondary institution outside Canada within the last 10 years, and it is assessed as equivalent to a Canadian degree through an ECA
  • You have at least 6 months of full time experience actively managing or owning a business, or the equivalent through a business incubator or accelerator
  • You hold a letter of recommendation from an AAIP approved designated agency
  • Your business plan, with projected financials, and your 10 minute pitch deck are ready for agency and Alberta review
  • You meet CLB 5 in each language skill, and your business sits in one of Alberta's key sectors, such as technology, energy, agriculture, tourism, life sciences, financial services, aerospace or pharmaceuticals
  • You can invest at least $100,000 in an urban centre or $50,000 in a regional area, and show settlement funds for your family size

This Stream Will Not Work If

  • You have no designated agency recommendation. The letter is a mandatory, pass or fail requirement, and the EOI cannot proceed without it
  • Your degree was completed more than 10 years ago, or it is not equivalent to a Canadian degree
  • Your business is outside the key sectors list. Sector fit is a pass or fail criterion, not a scoring preference
  • Your ownership would fall below 34% in an urban centre or 51% in a regional area
  • Your investment is below the minimum for your location, or your settlement funds fall short of the Low Income Cut Off based requirement
  • Your business partners are not Canadian citizens or permanent residents

The Reality

The Real Challenges Foreign Founders Face

The Agency Is A Real Interview

Agencies recommend founders they believe will succeed. They test market need, customer acquisition and financial realism. A plan written to satisfy a checklist, rather than to convince an investor minded reviewer, stops at this gate.

Urban Or Regional Changes The Math

Calgary and Edmonton mean a $100,000 minimum investment and 34% ownership. A regional area means $50,000 and 51%. The same start up is a different financial proposition depending on where you land it.

The Pitch Deck Is Pass Or Fail

A 10 minute, slides only presentation focused on what an investor wants to see is a mandatory minimum, not an optional extra. Founders who treat it as a summary of the business plan undersell the venture.

Settlement Funds Are Sized To The Town

The funds you must show depend on your family size and the population of the community where you will establish the business, based on the Low Income Cut Offs. It is a specific table, not a round number.

Why Files Fail

Why Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Files Stall Or Get Declined

No Recommendation Letter

Without a letter from an approved designated agency, the EOI does not meet a mandatory minimum. This is the most common reason files never start.

The Business Misses The Key Sectors

The plan or pitch deck must connect the business to one of the listed sectors: technology, aerospace, financial services, energy, agriculture, tourism, life sciences or pharmaceuticals. A good business in the wrong sector fails this criterion outright.

The Degree Is Too Old Or Not Equivalent

A degree completed more than 10 years before the EOI, or one the ECA does not equate to a Canadian degree, does not meet the education requirement.

Investment Or Funds Fall Short

The minimum investment for your location and the settlement funds for your family size are both pass or fail. Being close is the same as being short.

The Financials Do Not Survive Review

The agency's written assessment of your plan accompanies your Business Application. Projections that cannot be explained, priced or defended weaken the file at both stages.

How It Works

How The Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Process Runs

1

Designated Agency First

Approach one of the three approved agencies with your business plan and pitch deck. The agency assesses your readiness and, if it supports the venture, issues your letter of recommendation.

2

Submit Your EOI

You submit your Expression of Interest with the recommendation letter, your plan, pitch deck and Net Worth Worksheet. Alberta scores it on the 200 point grid.

3

Business Application And Launch

Top ranking candidates are invited to submit a Business Application, including the agency's written assessment report. You then come to Alberta on a work permit, launch the start up, and work toward nomination.

The Agency Decides First

In every other entrepreneur stream, the province is the first reader of your plan. Here, a designated agency reads it first, stakes its recommendation on it, and reports on it in writing to Alberta.

That changes how the plan should be written. It has to convince people whose business is judging start ups: a clear market, a believable path to customers, and numbers that hold together. We prepare founders for that conversation specifically.

Questions

Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream Questions, Answered

Which agencies can recommend me?

Alberta lists exactly three approved designated agencies: Elite Global Group Ltd. in Edmonton, Empowered Startups in Vancouver, and Platform Calgary in Calgary. A recommendation from any other organization does not meet this stream's requirements.

How is this different from the federal Start Up Visa?

They are different programs run by different governments. The Start Up Visa is a federal program administered by IRCC with its own designated organizations, and it leads directly to federal permanent residence. This stream is Alberta's program: it runs through Alberta's designated agencies, ends in a provincial nomination, and your business must be launched in Alberta. Some founders qualify for both, and the right first move depends on where the business will actually operate.

Can I apply from outside Canada?

Yes. This stream is designed for graduates who are outside Canada. Your investment must be in place before you come to Alberta, and you will launch the business here on a work permit while your permanent residence process runs.

Does my business have to be a technology company?

No, but it must connect to one of Alberta's key sectors: technology, aerospace, financial services, energy, agriculture, tourism, life sciences or pharmaceuticals. Sector fit is a pass or fail criterion, so it is checked before anything else in your plan.

What does the agency actually do?

The agency reviews your business plan for market need, customer acquisition, partnerships and financial realism, judges your readiness as a founder, issues the letter of recommendation if it supports you, provides a written assessment report for your Business Application, and connects you with networks, coaching and guidance while you launch.

Can you promise an agency will recommend me, or that Alberta will select me?

No, and no consultant honestly can. Agencies decide who they recommend and Alberta decides nominations. What we do is prepare your plan, deck and file so the agency sees a venture that fits its mandate and Alberta sees a score that reflects your full profile.
